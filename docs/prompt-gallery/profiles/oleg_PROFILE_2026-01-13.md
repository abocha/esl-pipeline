# 🧠 Comprehensive Student Profile: Oleg

**Date:** January 13, 2026
**Status:** Active (Post-Holiday Return) | **Level:** Beginner (A1 Emerging)

## A. Executive Summary

- **Student:** Oleg (35-37, Male, St. Petersburg). Coffee bar owner/barista.
- **Goal:** Survival English for travel. Secondary goal: Memory improvement.
- **Current State:** Oleg has successfully navigated the "To Be" vs. "Can" distinction. He recently returned from a 3-week break (Dec-Jan) with **surprisingly good retention**, validating his new habit of using flashcard apps.
- **Learning Mode:** Logical/deductive. Needs to translate to L1 (Russian) to anchor meaning. He responds well to antonym pairs (Fast/Slow) and visual cues.
- **Critical Win:** The introduction of the "ReWord" app has significantly improved his vocabulary retention, which was previously his biggest weakness.

## B. Performance Analysis

### 👍 Strengths

1.  **Retention Resilience:** Despite a 3-week gap between Lessons 5 and 6, he remembered core concepts (`Is` vs `Can`, basic verbs) better than expected.
2.  **Logical Grammar Application:** He grasps rules when they are binary/logical (e.g., `Is` = State, `Can` = Ability).
3.  **Antonym Mapping:** He learns vocabulary fastest when presented in opposing pairs (`Near` vs `Far`, `Fast` vs `Slow`).

### ⚠️ Recurring Challenges (The "Fix-It" List)

1.  **Pronunciation of "Silent E":** Specifically with the word **`Tired`**. He persistently tries to say "Ti-red". Also struggles with the "UGH" sound in `Laugh` (confusing it with `Love`).
2.  **State vs. Action Confusion:** While improved, he still occasionally produces "hybrid" errors like `He is run` or `He is fast run` instead of `He can run fast`.
3.  **Article Blindness:** He understands `a` (one) vs `the` (specific) conceptually when explained, but drops them consistently in speech (`Dog is in park`).
4.  **"There is" Structure:** This was briefly introduced but is not yet active. He tries to translate directly from Russian (e.g., "Park has dog").
